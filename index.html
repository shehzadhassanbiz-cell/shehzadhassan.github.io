Hand calculation

Ply stiffness from the material data (E1 = 121 GPa, E2 = 8.6 GPa, ν12 = 0.27, G12 = 4.7 GPa):

Q11 = E1/(1 − ν12ν21) = 121.6 GPa    Q22 = 8.65 GPa    Q12 = 2.33 GPa    Q66 = 4.7 GPa

Averaging the rotated ply stiffness over the stack (two plies at 0°, four at ±45°, two at 90°):

A11/h = (2·121.6 + 4·38.4 + 2·8.65)/8 = 51.8 GPa    Ex = (A11² − A12²)/A11 = 47.0 GPa

Peak strain at the hole, then the stress across the fibres in a 90° ply, including the Poisson narrowing:

εx = Kt·σnet/Ex = 0.0033    σ2 = Q22εx + Q12(−νxyεx) ≈ 26 MPa

Against a transverse tensile strength of 29 MPa, the 90° plies are at 90 % of their limit at 10 kN, so first ply failure is predicted at about 11 kN.

Model and discussion
  • Surface geometry meshed with layered shell elements; eight 0.25 mm plies defined in ACP with the 0° direction along the load.
  • Ansys is about 9 % more critical than the hand estimate because Tsai-Wu combines all ply stresses, while the hand check uses only the stress across the fibres.
  • First ply failure is matrix cracking, not rupture: the 0° fibres are at roughly 18 % of their strength at the same load.
  • Error caught by the hand check: the first run showed 88 mm of deflection where 0.33 mm was expected. The plies were stacked on one side of the reference surface, so the load acted 1 mm off-centre and bent the plate. A beam estimate of this bending gave 86 mm, confirming the cause before it was corrected.

A result is only as good as its check: the hand calculation exposed an 88 mm error and traced it to a 1 mm offset.

Free-surface flow over a bed ridge

Tools
OpenFOAM, FreeCAD, ParaView
Methods
Two-phase CFD, local mesh refinement, validation against flume experiments
Context
University of Oldenburg / Hochschule Emden-Leer, team of three, 2023

Water flowing over a ridge in a 16 m laboratory flume at 0.152 m³/s, simulated and compared point by point with measured water levels.

Experiment vs simulation

Model and discussion
  • Bed ridge modelled in FreeCAD; domain meshed with blockMesh and snappyHexMesh, refined where the lab showed strong turbulence; run in parallel on four cores.
  • Constant inflow of 0.152 m³/s at 0.335 m depth, as in the experiment.
  • The main mismatch is the position of the water-level dip. Likely causes are the coarse base mesh and turbulence modelling near the jump, plus reading errors in a highly turbulent lab flow. No mesh study was done at the time; this is now standard in my work.

Explaining where a model disagrees with an experiment is more useful than a perfect match.
